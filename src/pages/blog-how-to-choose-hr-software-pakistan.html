How to choose HR and payroll software in Pakistan

Most companies start looking for HR software the week after a bad payroll month. That's a terrible time to choose. Here's a calmer way to do it, with the questions worth asking and the traps worth avoiding.

{{tick}} WorkDesk team·31 October 2026·{{reading}} min read

If you're reading this, you probably run HR on some mix of Excel, a biometric machine and WhatsApp. It works until it doesn't. Someone leaves and takes the logic of the salary sheet with them. A late fine gets disputed and nobody can prove anything. The owner asks what overtime cost last quarter and the answer takes two days.

HR software fixes most of that, but only if you pick one that fits how your company actually works. Feature lists all look the same. The differences show up in the details, so that's where this checklist spends its time.

Start with the problem, not the feature list

Before you look at a single demo, write down the three things that hurt most today. Be specific. "Payroll takes four days because we copy attendance by hand" is useful. "We need better HR" is not.

Those three problems become your test. Any system you consider should solve them on a call, with your data or something close to it. If a vendor spends the demo on features you didn't ask about, that tells you something.

Local requirements that matter in Pakistan

International HR tools are often excellent and still awkward here. These are the details that tend to separate a good fit from a frustrating one:

  • Month-end payroll with deductions. Income tax withholding, provident fund, EOBI where it applies, advances, loans and fines. Ask to see a payslip with all of them on it.
  • Shift work. Morning, evening, night and rotating shifts, with overtime rules that differ by shift or day.
  • Attendance machines. If you already own biometric or card machines, check how their data gets into the system. Re-typing it defeats the purpose.
  • Multiple branches and cities. Different timings, holidays and managers for each location.
  • Local records. CNIC numbers and copies, next of kin, and names that get spelled several ways.
  • Public holidays. National and religious holidays, some of which aren't fixed until close to the date.
  • Mobile first. Many of your staff will use the system only on their phones. Test the app, not just the web version.

Tax slabs change with the federal budget, so the question isn't whether the software knows this year's rates. It's how easily you can update them, and whether the vendor helps when they change.

Questions to ask in the demo

Bring these and don't let the call end until each one has a clear answer.

  1. Can you show a late arrival turning into a fine on a payslip, using a rule like ours?
  2. What happens when attendance is corrected after payroll has been run?
  3. How does a manager approve leave from their phone, and what does the employee see afterwards?
  4. Can a manager see their own team without seeing salaries?
  5. How do we import our current employee sheet, and who does the work?
  6. Can we export all of our data if we leave? In what format?
  7. What does support look like after go-live: a ticket system, a phone number, a named person?

The second question is the most revealing. Every real payroll has corrections. A system that can't handle them cleanly will push you back into spreadsheets within three months.

How pricing usually works

Most HR software in Pakistan is priced per employee per month, sometimes with a minimum. Some vendors sell modules separately, so attendance and payroll might be priced differently from performance or recruitment. Before you compare numbers, line up:

  • What counts as an employee. Active staff only, or everyone ever entered?
  • Whether the mobile app costs extra.
  • Set-up, data import and training fees.
  • The price when you grow from 50 to 150 people.
  • Annual versus monthly billing, and what happens if you need to stop.

A cheaper monthly price with a large set-up fee and a one-year lock-in is often the more expensive option. Ask for a written quote that covers all of it.

Data, access and security

You're about to put salaries, CNIC numbers and bank details into someone else's system. It's fair to ask hard questions:

  • Who at the vendor can see our data, and why?
  • Is the connection encrypted, and how are backups handled?
  • Can we control access by role, down to who sees salaries?
  • Is there a history of who changed what?

A vendor who is relaxed and specific about these questions is usually a vendor who has thought about them.

Red flags

  • The demo uses only polished sample data and never touches a messy real case.
  • "We can customise that" is the answer to every question. Customisation is often slow and paid.
  • No clear way to export your data.
  • The mobile app is an afterthought.
  • Pricing only arrives after several calls.

A simple way to compare

Score each option from 1 to 5 on the things that matter to you, then weight them. Here's a starting point:

Solves our top three problems30%Shown live on the demo
Payroll with our deductions and fines20%A correct payslip from our rules
Mobile app for staff and managers15%Leave and approvals in a few taps
Set-up and support15%They import data and attend the first payroll
Total cost over two years10%Written quote, no surprises
