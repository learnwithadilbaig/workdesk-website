Moving HR from Excel to software, step by step

Excel is a great tool and a bad HR system. Switching is less painful than people expect, as long as you do it in the right order. This is the order.

{{tick}} WorkDesk team·31 October 2026·{{reading}} min read

Signs you've outgrown the spreadsheet

Nobody decides to run HR on Excel. It just happens: one sheet for employees, another for salaries, a folder of attendance exports, and a tab called "FINAL final v3". You've probably outgrown it if a few of these sound familiar:

  • Payroll takes more than a day, and most of that day is copying and checking.
  • Only one person really understands the salary sheet's formulas.
  • Leave balances are worked out when someone asks, not kept up to date.
  • Late fines get disputed and there's no easy way to show the records.
  • Managers approve things on WhatsApp and HR finds out later.

None of this means anyone has done a bad job. It means the company grew and the tools didn't.

Step 1: Clean up your employee master sheet

Your new system is only as good as the data you put into it. Before you import anything, make one sheet with one row per current employee and these columns at minimum:

  • Employee ID, full name as on CNIC, and CNIC number
  • Department, designation, branch and manager
  • Joining date and employment type (permanent, contract, probation)
  • Basic salary and each regular allowance as separate columns
  • Bank name and account number
  • Shift and leave entitlement

Look for duplicates, people who have left but are still listed, and salaries stored as text instead of numbers. This is boring work. It's also the work that prevents a wrong first payslip, which is the fastest way to lose everyone's trust in a new system.

Step 2: Write down your policies

Spreadsheets hide policy inside formulas and people's heads. Software needs it written down. Get these on paper, even if it's a single page each:

  • Working hours and shifts, including grace periods for late arrival
  • Leave types, yearly entitlements, carry-forward and encashment rules
  • Overtime: who can approve it, and at what rate
  • Fines and deductions, with the exact thresholds
  • Advances and loans, and how they're recovered

You'll often discover that two managers have been applying different rules. Settle that now, before the system applies one rule to everyone. If fines are part of your policy, our guide to a fair late-arrival policy will help.

Step 3: Pick a cut-over date

Choose the first day of a payroll month. Starting mid-month means splitting attendance between two systems, which is exactly the kind of mess you're trying to leave behind. Give yourself two to four weeks before that date for set-up.

Opening leave balances matter here. Work out each person's balance as of the cut-over date and import it, so nobody loses days they've earned.

Step 4: Import and check

Import the cleaned employee sheet, then check a sample by hand. Pick ten people across different departments and grades and compare every field. If those ten are right, the rest usually are too. If two are wrong, fix the cause, not just the two rows.

Step 5: Run one month in parallel

For the first month, run payroll in both the old spreadsheet and the new system, then compare the results person by person. Differences will appear. Most of them will turn out to be cases where the spreadsheet was quietly wrong, or where a manager applied a rule nobody wrote down.

Investigate every difference and decide which answer is correct. This single step does more for confidence than any amount of training.

Step 6: Train managers before staff

Employees will use the app for leave and payslips, and that part is usually intuitive. Managers are the ones who need to change habits: approving in the system instead of on WhatsApp, and assigning tasks with deadlines. Spend your training time on them. Once managers stop accepting requests by message, staff follow quickly.

Step 7: Switch off the old sheets

After a clean parallel month, make the old files read-only and store them somewhere safe. Keep them for audit, but stop updating them. Two live sources of truth is how companies end up back where they started.

Common mistakes

  • Importing messy data and planning to fix it later. Later never comes.
  • Copying every old exception into the new system. Use the switch to simplify.
  • Skipping the parallel run. It feels slow. It saves months.
  • Letting one person own the whole project. HR, finance and at least one manager should all be involved.

A quick checklist

  • Employee master sheet cleaned and checked
  • Policies written down and agreed
  • Cut-over date on the first day of a payroll month
  • Opening leave balances calculated
  • Sample of ten records checked after import
  • One payroll month run in parallel
  • Managers trained, old sheets made read-only
